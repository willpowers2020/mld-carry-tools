Key Concept

What exactly is a Triple Stack?

A "2DP carry" means a 2-digit pair (like 36) appears in one day's draw and then shows up again in the very next day's draw for the same state. It's one of the most observable short-term patterns in Pick 4. A Triple Stack takes that basic signal and compounds it — three separate momentum indicators firing at the same time, on the same state, on the same day.

The Three Conditions
1. Multi-Carry — 3 or more 2DP pairs carrying simultaneously
A single pair carrying is common (~77% of states have at least one on any given day). But when three or more pairs carry on the same state, that state has unusual structural overlap between consecutive draws. It means the winning number shared multiple digit relationships with the previous day — not just one coincidence, but a cluster of them.
2. Repeat Carry — at least one of those pairs also carried the day before
If pair 36 carried from Nov 7→8 AND also carries from Nov 8→9, it's a "repeat." The pair has now appeared in draws three days in a row. This isn't random noise — the digit combination is locked into the state's draw pattern and persisting across transitions.
3. Hot Pair — at least one pair has heat14 ≥ 4 (appeared 4+ times in the last 14 days)
"Heat" measures how frequently a pair has appeared recently. A pair that shows up 4 or more times in 14 days is running well above its expected frequency (~1.4 for any given pair). It's not just carrying — it's dominating the recent draw history. When a hot pair also carries and repeats, all three signals converge.
Why it matters: Any one of these conditions alone is fairly common. Multi-carry happens on a few states each day. Repeat carries happen on a few states each day. Hot pairs exist in every state. But when all three happen together — that's a Triple Stack, and it's rare. Across 182 observed Triple Stack events, the state carried at least one pair into the next day 83% of the time. That's the signal: the structure is self-reinforcing, and it almost always continues.
Real example: how Oregon got its 360 score
Oregon drew 6-1-2-3 at 1PM on Nov 8. That single number contains six 2-digit pairs: 12, 13, 16, 23, 26, 36.
The next day (Nov 9), Oregon drew 1-2-6-3 at 10PM. Same digits, reshuffled — same six pairs. All six carried. Plus three more pairs from the 4PM and 7PM draws. That's 9 multi-carries (condition 1).
Three of those pairs — 13, 17, 18 — had also carried from Nov 7→8. They were on their third consecutive day. That's 3 repeat carries (condition 2).
Every single one of the 9 pairs had heat14 ≥ 4. Pair 36 had heat 8 (appeared 8 times in 14 days). That's 9 hot pairs (condition 3).
The momentum score multiplies all three: 9 × (3+1) × (9+1) = 360. The median state scored 4. Oregon was 90 times the median.
Contrast: what a NON-Triple-Stack looks like
On the same day (Nov 8→9), Iowa had 2 pairs carry (below 3 — fails multi-carry), 1 repeat, and 1 hot pair. Score: 2 × (1+1) × (1+1) = 8. That's not a Triple Stack — it has a repeat and a hot pair, but only 2 carries instead of the minimum 3.
Rhode Island had 4 carries and 2 hot pairs, but zero repeats. Score: 4 × (0+1) × (2+1) = 12. No repeat carry means the structure just appeared — it isn't persisting across days. Also not a Triple Stack.
Triple Stack requires all three conditions simultaneously. That's what separates Oregon's 360 from every other state that day — and why the 83% continuation rate is so high: the carries aren't just present, they're overlapping, recurring, and frequent.
